How the model works

Honest label: this is a simplified classroom model. Its process equations are informed by published aquatic-ecosystem modelling (especially the US EPA AQUATOX technical documentation), but it is not AQUATOX, and most parameter values are illustrative teaching values, not measurements. It has been checked for internal consistency (it conserves matter) but has not been validated against a real aquarium. Do not use it to make animal-care decisions.

What is in the tank

Living pools (dry mass per litre): goldfish, algae, crop plants, decomposer bacteria, nitrifying bacteria. Non-living pools: detritus (dead matter, tracked as carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus), ammonium-N, nitrate-N, phosphate-P, dissolved oxygen, dissolved inorganic carbon. Each organism has a fixed proportion of C, N and P, so every process moves elements in exact amounts.

Process equations (simplified)

  • Producer growth (Monod + Liebig): growth = μmax × biomass × flight × min(fN, fP, fC) × θT−20, with fN = N/(KN+N) and fP = P/(KP+P). This structure matches AQUATOX 3.2 eqs. 52–56. Legović & Cruzado (1997) combine Monod uptake with Droop growth on internal nutrient quotas; our version drops the internal quota. Half-saturation constants are illustrative.
  • Light: a daily sine-shaped light curve; algae shade themselves (Beer–Lambert average over the water depth).
  • Algal death: background mortality plus extra mortality when light and nutrients are poor, following the form of AQUATOX's stress term.
  • Fish: eat food and graze algae → assimilate part → build tissue if N and P allow → respire the rest (CO₂ out, O₂ used) → excrete the extra N as ammonium and P as phosphate. Feces and uneaten food become detritus.
  • Decomposition: bacteria consume detritus (faster with oxygen, half-saturation 0.5 mg O₂/L as in AQUATOX), grow with 30 % efficiency and release CO₂, ammonium and phosphate (mineralization).
  • Nitrification: NH₄⁺ + 2 O₂ → NO₃⁻ … uses 4.57 g O₂ per g N. It transforms nitrogen — it does not remove it.
  • Denitrification: NO₃⁻ → N₂ gas, needs low oxygen and organic carbon. This does remove nitrogen from the water.
  • Oxygen: dO₂/dt = photosynthesis + reaeration − respiration − decomposition − nitrification; reaeration = Kreaer × 1.024T−20 × (O₂sat − O₂) (AQUATOX 3.2 eqs. 191, 198). O₂sat uses the Benson & Krause equation adopted by USGS.
  • Un-ionized ammonia: fraction NH₃ = 1/(1+10pKa−pH), pKa = 0.09018 + 2729.92/T (Emerson et al. 1975).
  • Crops: roots take nitrogen and phosphate from the water; leaves take CO₂ from and release O₂ to the air. Growth is limited by grow-bed space; harvests remove crops and their nutrients.

Carbon cycling vs. energy flow

Carbon atoms cycle: CO₂ → algae (photosynthesis) → fish or detritus → CO₂ (respiration, decomposition) → algae again. Energy flows: it enters as light (and as the chemical energy in fish food bought from outside), is passed along when organisms eat, and at every step most of it is released as heat by respiration. Heat cannot be used again by organisms, so energy must keep entering. The model tracks the two separately: grams of N, P and C in the mass balance, and kilojoules in the energy budget.

The ~10 % rule summarizes real food webs: on average about 10 % of the energy at one trophic level becomes biomass at the next (Lindeman 1942; Pauly & Christensen 1995 estimated a mean near 10 % for aquatic food webs). Real values vary widely. The model does not force 10 % — the fish's efficiency emerges from its digestion and respiration settings, so students can test the rule.

What is simplified or left out (compared with AQUATOX)

  • Fixed elemental composition of organisms (no Droop internal nutrient quotas).
  • One algae group and one fish species; no zooplankton, invertebrates or macrophytes.
  • Constant temperature and pH; pH does not respond to CO₂. Carbonate chemistry is reduced to one inorganic-carbon pool.
  • No sediment layer or sediment diagenesis; no phosphorus binding to sediments.
  • Well-mixed water; no stratification, flow, inflow or outflow (except optional water changes).
  • Fish mortality from low oxygen and un-ionized ammonia uses simple illustrative stress thresholds, not AQUATOX's time-to-death toxicity relationships. Real tolerance differs by species, size and acclimation.
  • No toxic chemicals, pathogens, disease or fish reproduction.

For teachers

Everything runs in the student's browser. There are no accounts, cookies, servers or API calls in the simulation. Student answers stay on the student's own device unless they download a report.

Preparing real data (optional, done once by the teacher)

  1. On a computer with Python 3.9 or newer (no extra packages needed), run
    python scripts/fetch_wqp.py list-sites --county "US:26:017"
    to list Bay County, Michigan monitoring locations and the characteristics measured at each.
  2. Pick a location, then download observations:
    python scripts/fetch_wqp.py download --site USGS-04157065 --start 2005-01-01 --end 2013-12-31
  3. The script writes a classroom CSV and a provenance JSON to data/datasets/ and adds them to data/datasets/index.json. Run python scripts/validate_data.py, then commit and push. Students will see the dataset in the Real Water Data tab.

The legacy Water Quality Portal profiles do not include USGS data added after March 11, 2024. Newer USGS discrete data are in the portal's WQX 3.0 profiles (use --profile wqx3) and in the USGS Samples API; continuous sensor data and daily values come from the modern USGS Water Data APIs (scripts/fetch_usgs.py). The legacy USGS WaterServices APIs are scheduled for decommissioning in early 2027, so this project does not use them.

Classroom tips

  • Ask students to predict before pressing Run. The Investigations tab has a prediction box for each investigation.
  • The grey baseline line lets students see the effect of their change compared with the default run.
  • Everything can be done with a keyboard: Tab between controls, arrow keys move sliders and read chart values.
  • The Chromebook-friendly design avoids animation: a 90-day run takes well under a second.
