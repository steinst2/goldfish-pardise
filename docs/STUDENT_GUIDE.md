# Student investigation guide

## Getting started

1. Open the link your teacher gave you. On the **Lab** tab, choose an ecosystem:
   **A** (an unmanaged fish tank), **B** (Waste-to-Plate aquaponics), or **Compare** (both at once with the same fish, food, light and aeration).
2. Set the sliders. **Predict what will happen before you press _Run experiment_.**
3. Read the results:
   * **Inspect a day** slider: moves the tank picture and the Metrics panel to any day.
   * **Charts**: hover, or Tab to a chart and press ← →, to read exact values. "Table" shows the numbers. "What am I seeing?" explains each chart.
   * **Where did the matter and energy go?**: nitrogen and carbon diagrams (grams moved by each process), the energy budget (kJ), and a mass-balance check.
4. The badge at the top tells you what kind of data you are looking at:
   * ◆ **OBSERVED DATA**: real measurements from a real place (only in the Real Water Data tab).
   * — **SIMULATED DATA**: the model with its default settings.
   * ✎ **STUDENT EXPERIMENT**: the model after *you* changed something. The grey line shows the default run for comparison.

## Doing an investigation

Open **Investigations** and pick 1–6. Each one has:

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| Question & background | Read them. |
| **Prediction** | Write what you think will happen and why, *before* running. |
| Procedure | Press **Set up this investigation**, then follow the steps. |
| **Results** | Press **Record my results from the current run** after each run. A table builds up. |
| **Evidence** | Which numbers or chart features support your claim? |
| **Scientific explanation** | Claim + reasoning (use the vocabulary: producer, consumer, decomposer, limiting factor, nitrification, respiration…). |
| **Reflection** | What are the model's limitations? What would you test next? |

Write your name at the top, then **Download lab report** (or **Print / save as PDF**) and submit it the way your teacher asks. Your answers are saved only in this browser on this device.

## The six investigations

1. **What causes an algal bloom?** Change nutrient loading and light; identify the limiting factor.
2. **Why do fish die during an ecosystem collapse?** Follow oxygen, decomposers and ammonia.
3. **Can aquaponics reduce nutrient accumulation?** Change crop density; compare dissolved nutrients and algae in A vs. B.
4. **Where does the energy go?** Calculate how much of the energy fish eat becomes fish, and compare with the ~10 % rule.
5. **Where does the matter go?** Trace nitrogen and carbon atoms; check that matter is conserved.
6. **Real-world water quality.** Explore real measurements from a monitoring station near Bay County, Michigan, and compare them, side by side, with your model.

## Remember

* A model is a tool for thinking, not a crystal ball. This one's equations come from published research, but its numbers are teaching values, and it has not been tested against a real tank.
* A river is not a fish tank. When you compare real data with the model, look for *patterns and scale*, and explain the differences.
